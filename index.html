ICE Detention Trends

Updated on May 29, 2025, to reflect newer data


Every year, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) subjects hundreds of thousands of people to civil immigration detention—a practice that is unjust, inhumane, and completely unnecessary. It does so under a veil of secrecy, sharing only limited, often error-prone statistics with the public. ICE’s failure to regularly release accurate, complete, and accessible data to the public enables it to operate its multi-billion-dollar detention network with impunity and little oversight.

To address this need, the ICE Detention Trends dashboard presents analysis by Vera that reveals an unprecedented level of detail about ICE detention populations—nationally and across the 1,357 facilities in which ICE detained people—on each day of the 16-year period from fiscal year 2009 through mid-fiscal year 2025 (October 1, 2008, through February 17, 2025). This includes a detailed picture of ICE detention during the first four weeks of President Trump’s second term, including people sent by the government to detention centers at Guantanamo Bay. Vera’s tool allows the public to see the kinds of information that ICE should share.

The federal government must end the harmful practice of civil immigration detention as a fundamental step toward ending mass incarceration in the United States. Until then, it should regularly release current, anonymized, person-level detention datasets. This would be a critical move toward making ICE operations transparent and enabling further research on detention and its impacts. Without data transparency, ICE’s operations—and their harms—will continue behind closed doors.
